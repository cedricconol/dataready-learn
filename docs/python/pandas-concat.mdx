# `concat`: Stacking DataFrames

`merge` joins two tables side by side on a key. `concat` puts one table on top of another table. Use `concat` when you have the files January.csv to December.csv and you need one table.

## How to stack rows

```python
pd.concat([orders.head(2), orders.tail(2)])
```

**Result:**

|   | order_id | customer_id | product_id | order_date | quantity | total_amount | status |
|---|----------|-------------|------------|------------|----------|--------------|--------|
| 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2024-01-05 | 1 | 999.0 | completed |
| 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2024-01-10 | 2 | 59.98 | completed |
| 28 | 29 | 8 | 1 | 2024-05-14 | 1 | NaN | completed |
| 29 | 30 | 6 | 6 | 2024-05-20 | 1 | 249.0 | completed |

Put the DataFrames in a **list**, inside square brackets. Pandas matches the columns by name and not by position. The two parts can therefore have different column orders.

Examine the index: 0, 1, 28, 29. Each part kept its original labels, so the result has missing numbers. Usually you want a new count:

```python
parts = [orders.head(2), orders.tail(2)]
pd.concat(parts, ignore_index=True)
```

**Result:**

|   | order_id | customer_id | product_id | order_date | quantity | total_amount | status |
|---|----------|-------------|------------|------------|----------|--------------|--------|
| 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2024-01-05 | 1 | 999.0 | completed |
| 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2024-01-10 | 2 | 59.98 | completed |
| 2 | 29 | 8 | 1 | 2024-05-14 | 1 | NaN | completed |
| 3 | 30 | 6 | 6 | 2024-05-20 | 1 | 249.0 | completed |

`ignore_index=True` numbers the rows again from 0. Use it when the old row labels have no meaning.

## The usual application

You rarely stack a table on itself. Usually you stack files:

```python
jan = pd.read_csv("january.csv")
feb = pd.read_csv("february.csv")
mar = pd.read_csv("march.csv")

quarter = pd.concat([jan, feb, mar], ignore_index=True)
```

Three months become one DataFrame and one analysis. This task is one of the most common pandas tasks for an analyst, and it needs one line of code.

## When the columns do not match

```python
full = products.head(2)
partial = products[["name", "price"]].tail(2)

pd.concat([full, partial])
```

**Result:**

|   | product_id | name | category | price |
|---|-----------|------|----------|-------|
| 0 | 1.0 | Laptop | Electronics | 999.0 |
| 1 | 2.0 | Mouse | Electronics | 29.99 |
| 4 | NaN | Notebook | NaN | 4.99 |
| 5 | NaN | Monitor | NaN | 249.0 |

The second part has no `product_id` column and no `category` column. Pandas therefore put `NaN` in those cells, and it showed no error. This behavior is convenient, but it is also a risk. A spelling error in a column name in one file creates an extra column that is almost empty, and pandas shows no error. After you stack files from different sources, check `.columns` and `.isna().sum()`.

## How to stack side by side

`axis=1` joins the columns instead of the rows:

```python
parts = [products[["name"]], products[["price"]]]
pd.concat(parts, axis=1).head(3)
```

**Result:**

|   | name | price |
|---|------|-------|
| 0 | Laptop | 999.0 |
| 1 | Mouse | 29.99 |
| 2 | Desk | 349.0 |

Pandas matches the rows by **index label** and not by a key. This method is fast, but it is also dangerous. If the two parts are not aligned, pandas joins the incorrect rows and shows no warning. If the tables have a key, use `merge`.

## `concat` or `merge`?

| Your data | Method |
|----------|------|
| The same columns and more rows (monthly files) | `concat` |
| The same rows and more columns (matched by a key) | `merge` |

:::note Coming from SQL?
`pd.concat([a, b])` is `UNION ALL`. For the behavior of `UNION`, which removes duplicates, add one step: `pd.concat([a, b]).drop_duplicates()`.
:::
